Introduction

Duke Ring — Marion “Duke” Ring, who uses he/him pronouns — is 42. Originally from Illinois, Duke lives in Springfield, Illinois. A person is not improved by accident. He is improved by the decision to take responsibility for the shape of his days, and to aim upward even when the path is narrow. This plan is that decision, written down: a way of setting his house in order and of walking, deliberately, from disorder toward a life that can bear weight.

He earned a B.S. in Finance from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in 2006. He built a career at J.P. Morgan and Neuberger Berman, and left J.P. Morgan around age 33. Since then he has worked full time building Waynewood Concepts, LLC, based in Treasure Island, Florida, and has had no W-2 employment since leaving. His driver’s license is still a Florida license; obtaining an Illinois license is a planned step. What has already been learned and built is not an epitaph. It is a foundation.

Needs

The needs are not abstract, and they will not wait for a more convenient hour. Duke needs housing now, in Springfield. He needs to increase his income substantially. He needs to do this by leveraging the skills he already has — in finance, in the company he has built, and in the consulting work already under way — rather than discarding them. A legal matter is factored into every part of this plan; it is set out, in plain language, in the Legal section. To look away from necessity is to invite chaos. To name it, and to act, is the beginning of order.

Approach

The work of correction is already under way. It is being carried out now, using AI and a team of section bots, not deferred until conditions improve. The pillars of the plan are an online MBA at the University of Illinois Springfield and at Southern Illinois University Carbondale, supported by federal student aid; career paths that include consulting, the IBEW Local 193 apprenticeship, AI engineer roles, and Mercor; housing in Springfield — a one- or two-bedroom, with the UIS on-campus option still being looked into; benefits, including Medicaid and SNAP; and the improvement of credit. None of this is a plea to be spared the load. It is the voluntary bearing of it, in a definite sequence, toward a more ordered, more productive, and more meaningful life.

Getting in versus pay

Two different questions

The ranking uses two separate ideas: the chance of getting in, and the pay you would earn once you are in. They are not the same number. Example: IBEW Local 193 is about a 65% chance of getting in on the current ranking (est.) — aptitude test, interview, ranked list, and limited spots. The chance of a qualifying test score is about 90% (est.), given an ACT 29 and passing Series 7, 63, and 65 each on the first try. Once you are accepted, pay is effectively guaranteed from day one, with set raises. Starting pay is about $40k–50k (est.).

Student loans cover housing: the SIUC or UIS aid refund pays rent, so every earnings figure on this page is earned on top of housing.

      Illinois residency
      Team research: you must live in Illinois before applying via ABE.       Self-employment income is reviewed, so bring the business's expense records and show that no owner draw is taken.
      Income and benefit size
      A one-person household with little or no income is likely under the limit. The maximum SNAP benefit for one person is $306 a month for federal fiscal year 2027 (October 2026–September 2027). IDHS sets the exact amount from your income and housing costs.
      ABAWD work rules
      Able-bodied adults 18–64 without a child 13 or under in the home must average 20 hours a week (80 hours a month) of work, an approved program, or qualifying community service, or they get only 3 months of SNAP in a 3-year period. Illinois’s current period ends December 31, 2026; a new period starts January 1, 2027. Paid work, including self-employment and day labor, counts.
      SNAP once you are a student
      Team research: half-time or greater MBA enrollment can block SNAP eligibility. When you are enrolled at least half-time in a graduate program, you also need a student exemption. The usual one is working 20 hours a week in paid employment. If you are self-employed, it is 20 hours a week and weekly earnings of at least the federal minimum wage times 20. Less than half-time enrollment does not trigger the student rule. Confirm each school’s half-time definition with financial aid and confirm student SNAP rules with IDHS.
      Medicaid for a single adult
      Illinois covers adults 19–64 under the ACA Adult category if income is at or below 138% of the federal poverty level (about $1,835 a month for one person in 2026; confirm with IDHS). Assets are not counted. Apply on the same ABE application.
      Medicaid work rules in 2027
      Starting January 1, 2027, ACA Adult applicants and renewals must meet a work requirement or an exemption. Half-time school enrollment meets it, as does monthly income of $580 or more, or 80 hours a month of work, volunteering, or a work program. Team research: applying for Medicaid by 5 p.m. on December 31, 2026, delays the 2027 work rules for new applicants, so applying before then is simpler.

      IBEW Local 193 and a record

      A felony conviction is not an automatic bar. The NECA-IBEW Local 193 JATC looks at the aptitude test and interview. You will be tested before hire and at random afterward, as the JATC requires. Some job sites (prisons, airports, power plants, military bases, some hospitals) run deeper checks and can close those particular jobs, but not the trade. Probation may require permission to travel and a valid driver’s license. A city electrical license in Illinois is usually not automatically barred by a conviction.

      Transferring supervision to Illinois

      Under the Interstate Compact, Illinois must accept a Tennessee transfer if Tennessee agrees and you are an Illinois resident (one continuous year before sentencing) or have willing family here plus a job or other means of support. Otherwise it is discretionary. ICAOS Rule 3.101.

      Travel and schedules

      Leaving the state usually needs permission, which favors remote consulting and local work. Self-employed and contract work can flex around reporting appointments; an apprenticeship schedule is fixed, so ask early.

      Ask about judicial diversion

      Ask your defense attorney whether Tennessee judicial diversion is available. If it is and it ends in a dismissal, that is the highest-leverage step for every path on this page.

      Illinois hiring protections

      Employers may not use an arrest or charge that has not led to a conviction to refuse to hire you (775 ILCS 5/2-103). After a conviction, they may reject you only if the offense is substantially related to the job or poses an unreasonable safety risk, with written notice and time to respond (775 ILCS 5/2-103.1). Employers with 15 or more employees cannot ask about criminal history until an interview or a conditional offer (820 ILCS 75). These rules cover work in Illinois; out-of-state remote employers may follow different law.

      Certificate of Good Conduct later

      Illinois can grant a Certificate of Good Conduct for an out-of-state felony after 2 years of good conduct. It can lift some Illinois job or licensing bars; it does not hide the record. 730 ILCS 5/5-5.5.

      • Air traffic control: applicants must start before age 31.
      • Federal security clearance and defense work: a recent conviction and active probation make it very unlikely.
      • Commercial pilot: large training costs and years before good pay, plus FAA and airline screening.
      • Truck driving with a CDL: open to records, but the U.S. median is $58,640, far below the goal.

            Under the Interstate Compact, Illinois must accept a Tennessee transfer if Tennessee agrees and you are an Illinois resident (one continuous year before sentencing) or have willing family here plus a job or other means of support. Otherwise it is discretionary. ICAOS Rule 3.101.

            Leaving the state usually needs permission, which favors remote consulting and local work. Self-employed and contract work can flex around reporting appointments; an apprenticeship schedule is fixed, so ask early.

            Ask about judicial diversion

            Ask your defense attorney whether Tennessee judicial diversion is available. If it is and it ends in a dismissal, that is the highest-leverage step for every path on this page.

            Illinois hiring protections

            Employers may not use an arrest or charge that has not led to a conviction to refuse to hire you (775 ILCS 5/2-103). After a conviction, they may reject you only if the offense is substantially related to the job or poses an unreasonable safety risk, with written notice and time to respond (775 ILCS 5/2-103.1). Employers with 15 or more employees cannot ask about criminal history until an interview or a conditional offer (820 ILCS 75). These rules cover work in Illinois; out-of-state remote employers may follow different law.

            Certificate of Good Conduct later

            Illinois can grant a Certificate of Good Conduct for an out-of-state felony after 2 years of good conduct. It can lift some Illinois job or licensing bars; it does not hide the record. 730 ILCS 5/5-5.5.

            • Returning to FINRA-registered finance: a felony conviction is a statutory disqualification for 10 years.
            • Air traffic control: applicants must start before age 31.
            • Federal security clearance and defense work: a recent conviction and active probation make it very unlikely.
            • Commercial pilot: large training costs and years before good pay, plus FAA and airline screening.
            • Truck driving with a CDL: open to records, but the U.S. median is $58,640, far below the goal.
