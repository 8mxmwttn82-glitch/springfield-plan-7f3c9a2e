Getting in versus pay

Two different questions

The ranking uses two separate ideas: the chance of getting in, and the pay you would earn once you are in. They are not the same number. Example: IBEW Local 193 is about a 65% chance of getting in on the current ranking (est.) — aptitude test, interview, ranked list, and limited spots. The chance of a qualifying test score is about 90% (est.), given an ACT 29 and passing Series 7, 63, and 65 each on the first try. Once you are accepted, pay is effectively guaranteed from day one, with set raises. Starting pay is about $40k–50k (est.).

    36 questions, 51 minutes, after a short break

    About two and a half hours
    Qualifying score
    A 4 or higher on the 1–9 stanine scale is the commonly published cutoff to be invited to interview. The Electrical Training Alliance applying booklet says you are told whether you received a qualifying score; exact scores are often not provided. Local 193 does not publish a different cutoff on its site. Confirm with the JATC.
    Retest wait

    Day-of rules
    Bring a photo ID. No calculator. No penalty for guessing; the score is the number of items you answer correctly. Plan to finish both tests in one session.

          IBEW Local 193 aptitude test

          Jobs & Income Agent·Beginning soon

          The NECA-IBEW Local 193 JATC uses the standard Electrical Training Alliance (NJATC) aptitude test battery for inside-wireman applicants. Official materials describe two parts only: Algebra and Functions, and Reading Comprehension. There is no mechanical-reasoning section on this battery. A higher qualifying score generally improves your place on the ranked list, together with the interview, but it does not guarantee a spot. Openings, the interview, and a pre-enrollment physical and drug screen still matter. On November 23, 2026, Jobs & Income Agent completed a requirements summary, a document checklist, and an aptitude study plan.

          Algebra and Functions
          33 questions, 46 minutes
          Reading Comprehension
          36 questions, 51 minutes, after a short break
          Session length
          About two and a half hours
          Qualifying score
          A 4 or higher on the 1–9 stanine scale is the commonly published cutoff to be invited to interview. The Electrical Training Alliance applying booklet says you are told whether you received a qualifying score; exact scores are often not provided. Local 193 does not publish a different cutoff on its site. Confirm with the JATC.
          Retest wait
          A full three months (90 days) must pass before you may retake the test. The Electrical Training Alliance applying booklet also refers to a six-month rule after each subsequent retake. Confirm the Local 193 rule when you apply. Testing early can invalidate the score.
          Day-of rules
          Bring a photo ID. No calculator. No penalty for guessing; the score is the number of items you answer correctly. Plan to finish both tests in one session.
          Local 193 test date
          Not yet published on the JATC site. Applications are accepted year-round; the test is scheduled after you apply. https://springfieldjatc193.org/apply-now

          Official practice: sample Algebra and Functions and Reading Comprehension questions and an answer key at https://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/SamplePage/PreparingfortheTest. The applying booklet, with timing and retest rules, is at https://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/SamplePage/ApplyingforApprenticeship. The Alliance also offers an Online Tech Math course to refresh industry math at https://www.electricaltrainingalliance.org/learning/onlinetechmath. Previous electrical knowledge is not required.
